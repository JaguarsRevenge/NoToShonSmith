2 · The books

Late, and wrong

Every Washington county must file an annual financial report with the State Auditor within 150 days of the end of its year. Chelan County filed its 2023 report 398 days late. The State Auditor found that "County leadership did not dedicate the necessary resources to submit the annual report on time and did not respond to our Office's outreach efforts." That report is addressed to the Board of Commissioners.State Auditor

The reports the county did file had serious errors. The State Auditor found a "material weakness" in how the county prepares its financial statements in both 2022 and 2023:

2022 audit

A $24 million fund left off the books

The county left a $24 million investment trust fund out of its statements and overstated its road-fund inventory by about $950,000.State Auditor

MATERIAL WEAKNESS
2023 audit

A $7.1 million federal grant missed

The county left a $7,135,749 federal grant off its list of federal spending, misstated ten other grants, and overstated road-fund inventory by $1,204,600.State Auditor

MATERIAL WEAKNESS

Both years, the State Auditor gave the same cause: the county "had not filled the position responsible for preparing the financial statements." The errors were corrected before the audits were finished.

In 2025 the elected County Auditor, Skip Moore, asked the board to fund that position. The board put off the decision three times in one month,Wenatchee World then funded it at $59,723 against his $112,789 request. Moore said he would not run again, citing the board's repeated refusals to give him staff.KPQ He retired this April.KPQ Smith chaired the board through all of it.

In its written answer to the State Auditor, the county promised that "the County Commissioners are committed to meeting the deadlines starting in 2025."State Auditor The chair of the board is the one who answers for that promise.

3 · The squeeze

Deficits first. Then a hiring freeze.

Years of deficit budgets come due. On November 3, 2025, Smith and the other two commissioners signed a letter freezing all county hiring through the end of 2026. "We understand this decision may impact departments and teams," it told department heads.Chelan County

The cuts reached Chelan Douglas CASA, the volunteer advocates who speak for foster children in court. Its county funding was cut 20%. When a reporter asked why, Hawkins answered. Smith did not respond.Dominick Bonny

By August the Sheriff was telling the board, "Certainly, I recognize our employees being overwhelmed," and warning of burnout and people leaving.Ward Media The County Auditor had already quit over staffing.

Smith said it himself at that August meeting: "Losing a person is a lot more expensive than supporting a person."Ward Media He is right. But the budgets he moved and seconded are how the county ended up in a hiring freeze.

Clint Strand wants "a better experience for you working with the county, and for the folks working inside the county."Strand

4 · Who pays

A tax break for 90 owners. The rest of us cover it.

Chelan County's Public Benefit Rating System cuts property taxes for landowners who keep land as open space. Until this June it was the only program in Washington that could cut a landowner's taxes by up to 100%, and its rules had not changed since 2014.Chelan County

~90owners get the break
192properties enrolled
$250Kin tax breaks every year

Chelan County's figures, July 2026. The total covers every district that taxes the land: schools, fire, roads and the county itself.

That money doesn't go away. Most property-tax districts collect a set dollar amount, so when a few owners pay less, everyone else in those districts pays more. Smith knows it. When the board finally capped the break at 75% this June, he called it "a more equitable system for the taxpayers who are making up the difference."Chelan County He had been a commissioner for three and a half years before that fix, which came in his re-election year, and he signed it as Chair.Resolution 2026-51

The break lowers the land's taxable value while it stays in the program; it is not a permanent exemption.

6 · The campaign

This year, you can't see who's paying

In 2018 and 2022 Smith reported every contribution: $32,549 and $33,154 raised. For 2026 he chose "mini reporting," which caps a campaign at $7,000 and requires no report of who gave. That is legal. It also means voters cannot see who is funding the chair of the county board.PDC dataPDC rules
